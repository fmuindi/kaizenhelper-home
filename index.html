Our founding story

We Saw It Happen. Again. And Again.

Working with organizations across Kenya and East Africa, we encountered a pattern that troubled us deeply, and eventually drove us to build something different.

Every organization we worked with had started with a clear mission: a specific, meaningful problem they set out to solve. Whether improving factory output, reducing waste, or delivering better service to customers. The founding problem was real, urgent, and personal.

But as they grew, something shifted. Daily fires took over. Leadership attention moved to quarterly reviews, inbox management, and operational noise. The founding problem, the very reason the company existed, quietly faded into the background.

We call this Problem-Solving Drift. It's not a failure of leadership or talent. It's a failure of system. Without a structured way to capture, track, and resolve what matters most, even the best teams lose their way.

"Companies don't fail because they run out of money. They fail because they stop solving the problem they were founded to solve." — Kaizen Helper, founding principle
