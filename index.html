Our founding story

We Saw It Happen. Again. And Again.

Working with companies across Kenya and East Africa, we encountered a pattern that troubled us deeply — and drove us to build something different.

Every organization we worked with had started with a clear mission: a specific, meaningful problem they set out to solve. Whether it was improving factory output, reducing waste, or delivering better service to their customers — the founding problem was real, urgent, and personal.

But as they grew, something shifted. Daily fires took over. Leadership attention moved to quarterly reviews, inbox management, and operational noise. The founding problem — the very reason the company existed — quietly faded into the background.

We call this Problem-Solving Drift. It's not a failure of leadership or talent. It's a failure of system. Without a structured way to capture, track, and resolve the problems that matter most, even the best teams lose their way.

"Companies don't fail because they run out of money. They fail because they stop solving the problem they were founded to solve."

Problem-solving drift

Teams shift from proactive problem-solving to reactive firefighting. The founding mission fades as daily survival takes over.

No structured tracking

Problems get discussed in meetings but never formally captured, assigned, or followed through to resolution. They simply disappear.

The same issues repeat

Without root cause analysis, recurring problems resurface every quarter — draining time, money, and morale, with no end in sight.
